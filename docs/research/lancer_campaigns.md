# LANCER campaigns: structure and patterns

Research notes for the procedural GM (`src/story/`, `data/game/story/`). This is our own summary
of public material: product pages, reviews, developer logs, podcasts, and the free core book.
It records **patterns only**. No plots, characters, names or text from any module or anyone's
campaign go into the game. The paid modules weren't read in full, only their public
descriptions and reviews. Where this disagrees with the core book, the book wins
([CANON.md](../CANON.md)).

## 1. What the core book itself prescribes (canon)

- **Missions are the unit of play** (Core p.40): a clear goal, clear stakes, and urgency. "Without
  player intervention, the outcome will be radically different, usually for the worse."
- **Every mission has the same frame** (p.40-44): briefing (the situation, the goal, the stakes) →
  preparation → reserves → boots on the ground → debrief (level up, talk it over) → downtime.
- **Goals and the group's goal can differ** (p.42). The players might secretly work against their
  patron. Goals can also change mid-mission when new information turns up (p.43). That is the
  book's own license for twists.
- **Stakes are best phrased as questions** (p.43): "Will the squad save X from Y? Will they learn who's
  paying Y?" Stakes can be personal and tied to a pilot's background.
- **The book's d20 goal table** (p.42) covers escort, answering an SOS, retrieval, investigating a
  tip, escorting ordnance, defense, exploring a derelict, demolishing infrastructure, a
  diversion, assassination, assault, boarding, a beachhead, smuggling, hunting rival pilots,
  covering an evacuation, rescue/extraction, securing a location, liberation, and stopping an
  incoming attack.
- **"Boots on the ground" usually doesn't start in combat** (p.44). Start just before it, with a
  little time to establish the situation.
- **Narrative play is resolved with skill checks** (p.45-48). If no mechs are involved, a fight is
  "almost always" resolved narratively. Consequences have kinds: harm, time, resources,
  collateral, position, effect (p.46). Skill challenges cover group efforts, for example "traversing
  the wastes: failure means you arrive with no supplies" (p.47).
- **Downtime** is structured actions plus freeform roleplay, and the GM can reward freeform
  bonding (p.50).
- **Setting levers** (p.10-11, p.21): Union and the Core, a dangerous galaxy outside it
  (rebellions, insurrections, piracy, wars), frontier colonies that differ greatly from core worlds,
  corpro-states (IPS-N with its anti-piracy Albatross ties, p.126; SSC, HORUS, Harrison Armory), the
  Karrakin Trade Baronies (p.92), NHPs and cascade (p.102-107), and mercenaries as a common
  lancer career (p.10, p.22).

## 2. Official modules: public structure

|---|---|
| Operation Solstice Rain | LL0-2, **2 missions, 6 combat scenes**, one city. Trouble breaks out during tense diplomacy between two rival nations on a long-isolated world, and the squad is sent on a rescue. Praised as the best first campaign (tighter cast, better encounters, strong GM advice); some find the fights harder than intended for newcomers. |
| Operation Winter Scar | LL2-4 sequel, 2 missions, 6 scenes. Insertion behind the lines, link-up with local resistance, and a hunt for a superweapon as a dispute escalates into open war. Adds a tank NPC class and a marine template. |

| Dustgrave | LL2-4, 2 missions, 9 combat scenes, some branching. A missing NHP and an investigation through a dust-buried port city, with gangs, black ops and a hidden threat. Each mission alternates **combat / narrative beat / one downtime**: Part 1 has 3 combats, 4 beats and 1 downtime; Part 2 has 4 combats (1 optional), 5 beats and 1 downtime. Praised for its characters, faction mechanics and branching. |
| Siren's Song: A Mountain's Remorse | LL1-3, one self-contained mission. A key NHP vanishes into hostile terrain, the rescue team sent after it goes silent, and the squad goes in. The theme is the gap between a power's ideals and its practice. |

## 3. Lancer Tactics (video game reference)

- The campaign is an **anthology**: loosely connected arcs, each "a handful of battles and
  downtimes" that develop a situation and its characters.
- Between battles: repair, level up, visual-novel conversations with portraits, light
  choose-your-own-adventure, then pick and launch the next fight. (The devlog compares it to
  Banner Saga and the Game Boy Advance Fire Emblem games.)
- A module editor stitches combats, downtime and narrative beats together with NPC arcs,
  ticking clocks, branching paths and triggers.

Sources: [devlog: downtimes and module editor](https://wick.itch.io/lancer-tactics/devlog/779542/downtimes-module-editor-water-temple),
[Lancer Tactics on itch.io](https://wick.itch.io/lancer-tactics).

## 4. What tables report (actual plays, podcasts, forums)

- **Combat dominates session time**: one fight per session (2-4 hours) is common, so every mission
  should give each fight a clear purpose and keep the scenes between fights short and punchy
  ([EN World thread](https://www.enworld.org/threads/is-lancer-any-good.701686/),
  [RPG Lessons Learned, part 2](https://rpgllpodcast.castos.com/episodes/lancer-part-2-campaign-update-lancer-operation-solstice-rain)).
- **Railroading complaints**: modules that walk players from scene to scene feel thin, while
  branching paths and player-set goals are praised (Dustgrave, the review above).
- **What players love**: pilot identity (portraits, triggers, building pilots), the grid fights,
  rookies becoming legends, recurring rivals, and finales where one pilot's arc pays off
  ([a review session with new players](https://batts.substack.com/p/building-a-time-machine-to-review),
  [RPG Lessons Learned finale](https://rpgllpodcast.castos.com/episodes/lancer-part-3-campaign-finale-lancer)).
- **Downtime engagement varies**. Some groups ignore it, so downtime should come with prompts and
  concrete offers from people (contacts, jobs), not a bare menu
  ([review session](https://batts.substack.com/p/building-a-time-machine-to-review)).
- **Actual-play series** (patterns only): [Bring Your Own Mech](https://chartable.com/podcasts/bring-your-own-mech)
  (pilots thrown together "by circumstance, destiny, and credits": a mercenary crew),
  [Zero.Blue.Orion](https://rephonic.com/podcasts/zeroblueorion-mecha-lancer-rpg-actual-play)
  (comedic anime tone, preventing a disaster), and
  [Dungeons + Drama Nerds](https://dungeonsanddramanerds.podbean.com/e/the-prince-of-diagnostics)
  (the classic choice: fight a rival group or answer a distress flare).
- The user's favorite channel, [B-Tier Wizard](https://www.youtube.com/@BTierWizard), is about how
  frames play, not story structure ([videos.md](videos.md)).

## 5. Patterns we use

1. **Two to five mission arcs**. Each arc is a situation that escalates, with a reveal halfway
   and a confrontation at the end. Campaigns are anthologies of arcs at different hubs.
2. **Missions alternate beats**: briefing → (journey or narrative beat) → fight → (twist) → fight
   → debrief. Keep beats short; one downtime between missions.
3. **Two sides plus the squad**: most situations are a conflict between two powers (a corpro
   vs. a local government, colonists vs. the charter company, two noble houses, raiders vs. a
   shipping line, an isolated world split over joining Union). The squad's choices move both
   standings: helping one costs you with the other.
4. **Twists come from the book's "the goal changes mid-mission"**: the patron lied, the target is a
   person, a third party arrives, an enemy wants to defect, civilians are where they shouldn't
   be, the real objective is elsewhere.
5. **People as levers**: a patron, a rival, an informant, a compromised official, someone with a
   secret. NPCs want things that become jobs, and favors pay out as reserves (Core p.40-43,
   p.53-55).
6. **Hazard and logistics are narrative**: crossing hostile ground is a skill challenge unless
   the squad brought Supplies (Core p.47, p.51). Lost time is a consequence (p.46). No survival
   meter.
7. **Recurring rivals**: a named enemy squad shows up again and again across an arc.
8. **Pilot hooks**: stakes tied to backgrounds (p.43). A job near a pilot's past matters more.

These become: `data/game/story/hooks.json` (more templates, stakes as questions),
`twists.json`, `conflicts.json`, `arcs.json`, and NPC archetypes with secrets in
`data/game/npcs/tables.json`. All the text in them is our own.
